## 10.2 New cases

### Wins built on reading documents nobody else read carefully

1. **Ackman/General Growth 2008–10 (V entry, SI outcome):** first tranche **Nov 13, 2008 at
   $0.35/share** (V, the 13D/A purchase schedule itself); ~7.5% of common plus swap exposure;
   thesis — liquidity problem, not solvency: assets > claims, so Chapter 11 could leave equity
   intact. GGP filed April 2009 (largest US RE bankruptcy then); Pershing provided $375M DIP;
   emerged Nov 2010 with creditors paid in full and equity alive; Howard Hughes spun to holders.
   **Outcome discipline:** ~$60M equity → ~$1.6bn (~77x) is the defensible figure (SI); the
   "$2.6bn+" versions in circulation are contaminated — $2.6bn traces to Pershing's *Canadian
   Pacific* trade. Total including swaps/DIP/warrants: U.
2. **Tepper/Appaloosa financials Feb–Mar 2009 (V thesis doc, SI outcome):** the edge was the
   **fine print of Treasury's Feb 2009 CAP white paper** (V — document public): government
   preferred would convert to common at ~10% discount to pre-Feb-9 prices, i.e., Treasury was
   implicitly buying common *above* market — no nationalization wipeout. Bought BAC ~$3, C ~$1,
   distressed bank paper; >$2.5bn deployed (SI). Fund ~+120% net 2009, ~$7bn profit (SI, audited
   returns private).
3. **Ackman/MBIA short 2002–09 (V report, SI outcome):** the 66-page "Is MBIA Triple-A?" (public)
   against ~140:1 monoline leverage; ~6–7 years of carry, an investigation *of Ackman*, and a
   rising stock before the 2008 collapse; ~$1.1bn booked by end-2008 (SI). With Allied (case 27):
   documented shorts pay on 5–7 year horizons, not quarters — Phase 2's short-economics finding
   at trade level.
4. **Klarman/Baupost Lehman claims 2011–14 (MS/SI, exact P&L U):** >$450M face bought 2012,
   ~14% of book per a Baupost letter excerpt; LBHI third-party recovery ~31% (V, NY Fed) against
   mid-teens entry prices flagged by practitioners — roughly a double before time value (SI
   arithmetic). The best-documented Baupost trade — and still mostly U, which is the honest
   summary of the entire firm.
5. **Greenblatt/Marriott–Host spin 1993 (MS, the book's own worked example):** Aug 1993 calls at
   $3.125 → $7.75 intrinsic post-spin (+148% in ~2 months); thesis = institutions must dump the
   indebted "toxic waste" co regardless of price while insider incentives (Bollenbach to Host)
   signaled value. Not independently audited (U at trade level), but the spin-off anomaly it
   demonstrates is peer-reviewed (Cusatis–Miles–Woolridge 1993, JFE) — the forced-seller family
   Phase 3 ranked #1 for retail.
6. **Burry/GameStop 2019–20 (V filings, WI profit):** public letters (V) demanded the full $238M
   buyback on a ~$300M market cap company he'd bought around $2–4 (2019 tranches; the 2018
   tranche ~$12.6) — explicitly noting extreme short interest. The buybacks shrank the float and
   mechanically enabled the later squeeze. **Scion sold everything in Q4 2020 at ~$10–20** —
   before the January 2021 spike to $483. Right thesis, right catalyst, payoff accrued to others
   (~$100M+ profit, WI estimate; the $1.5bn counterfactual is exactly that).

### Wins on franchise/valuation judgment

7. **Buffett/Apple 2016–18 (V):** initiated Q1 2016 (~$1.07bn, ~$109/sh quarter-end — fill
   prices unpublished) **by Combs or Weschler, not Buffett** (MS); built to **$36.0bn cost basis**
   (V, 2018 AR) — the study's largest documented example of scaling into conviction over
   ~8 quarters. Exit side already on the ledger (Phase 7).
8. **Buffett/PetroChina 2002–07 (V/MS):** $488M for 1.3% at ~$37bn implied value vs his ~$100bn
   appraisal (MS, 2007 letter); sold in ≥6 HKEx-disclosed tranches Jul–Oct 2007 for ~$4bn
   ($1.2bn US tax paid — V, letter); "100% a valuation decision"; admitted selling "too soon" —
   then the stock collapsed in 2008. The clean case that a valuation exit *should* look early.
9. **Buffett–Munger/BYD 2008–25 (V entry/filings, WI total):** $230–232M for 10% at HK$8
   (Sept 2008, Munger's push); first trim Aug 2022, below 5% by Jul 2024, **carried at zero in
   BHE's Q1 2025 filing** — ~17-year hold, ~$10bn estimated total (WI — below-threshold sales
   unobservable). Documented maximum for "let the winner run inside a permanent-capital book."
10. **Lynch/Chrysler 1982 & Fannie Mae (MS — his own books, never position-audited):** Chrysler
    bought ~$6 into priced-for-bankruptcy, built to **5% of Magellan — the regulatory maximum**;
    ~15-bagger. Fannie Mae = Magellan's biggest single profit ($500M per *Beating the Street*).
    Both categories (turnaround, stalwart-gone-cheap) sized to their caps — Lynch's category
    system was a sizing grammar, not just a taxonomy.
11. **Icahn/Netflix 2012–15 (V entry, SI outcome):** 13D Oct 31, 2012 — ~10% for ~$321M
    (shares + calls); NFLX adopted a poison pill; sourced by **Brett Icahn**, who objected when
    Carl halved it in Oct 2013; full exit June 2015, ~$1.9–2bn (~7x, SI). **Paired control:**
    Icahn/Blockbuster — proxy fight 2005, ~$180M loss, "worst investment I ever made" (MS) —
    same investor, same sector-adjacent bet, opposite structural read.
12. **Druckenmiller/NVDA 2022–24 (V filings, MS commentary):** bought Q4 2022 on the AI thesis
    (an associate flagged it); top holding; fully out by Q2 2024 at "$800–950" pre-split (MS);
    then on camera Oct 2024: selling was **"a big mistake… I'm licking my wounds."** A ~3x
    realized and still self-scored as an exit error — the exit problem has no upper bound.

### Losses and structural cases

13. **Ackman/Herbalife 2012–18 (V events, U exact loss):** ~$1bn short at ~$45–50, the 342-slide
    presentation, target zero; Icahn took the other side at ~26% peak; FTC settled July 2016 for
    $200M *without* a shutdown; Nov 2017 converted to puts capping risk at ~3% of capital (MS);
    exited Feb 2018. Loss "nearly $1bn" (press) vs ~$760M (secondary) vs smaller (MS) —
    **U, never published.** Icahn's documented other side: ~$1.3bn profit, fully out May 2021
    (SI). 2024 coda: HLF -32% to a 14-year low — directional vindication *six years after the
    forced exit*, which is worth nothing. Being right late is indistinguishable from being wrong.
14. **Soros–Druckenmiller tech 1999–2000 (MS/SI — the "knowing better" case):** shorted tech
    early 1999, covered for ~$600M loss; flipped long, Quantum +35% for 1999; bought ~$6bn more
    near the March 10, 2000 top; **lost ~$3bn in ~6 weeks**; left April 2000. His own verdict
    (MS): *"I didn't learn anything. I already knew that I wasn't supposed to do that. I was
    just an emotional basket case and couldn't help myself."* The study's cleanest documented
    proof that knowledge does not bind behavior — only mechanical pre-commitment does
    (Phase 9 seed #5, Phase 15 requirement).
15. **Porsche/VW squeeze Oct 2008 (V):** Porsche's Sunday disclosure — 42.6% ordinaries + 31.5%
    via cash-settled options = 74.1% access; with Lower Saxony's 20%, free float ~5% vs ~12%
    shorted. VW €200 → >€1,000 intraday, briefly the world's most valuable company; ~$30bn
    aggregate short losses (SI, never audited); Merckle suicide Jan 2009 (V); Porsche executives
    eventually acquitted (V). **Twelve years before GME, with derivatives instead of Reddit** —
    the float-mechanics-override-fundamentals doctrine (Phase 6 short controls) was already
    fully documented. Melvin's 2021 lesson was available in 2008.

## 10.3 Pattern extraction (feeds Phase 11)

1. **The recurring edge in the win column is documentary, not informational:** Tepper read the
   CAP term sheet; Ackman read the bankruptcy math on GGP; Elliott read the pari passu clause;
   Greenblatt read the spin-off incentive filings; Burry read the buyback capacity against the
   float. Public documents, carefully read, before consensus — the single most retail-accessible
   edge pattern in the study, and it matches Phase 3's "free/official data" verdict.
2. **Horizon asymmetry is structural:** panic-bottom longs paid in months (Tepper, GGP);
   fundamental shorts took 5–7 years and personal cost (MBIA, Allied) — and squeeze mechanics
   (VW, GME) can kill the short before vindication arrives. Sizing and financing must match the
   horizon class, or the trade dies of duration even when right.
3. **The exit problem is universal and unsolved at every skill level:** PetroChina ("too soon,"
   then vindicated), NVDA ("big mistake"), Burry-GME (sold before the payoff), Icahn halving
   Netflix over his son's objection. Phase 7's conclusion strengthens: since even the best
   documented exits look wrong at *some* horizon, the only defensible standard is fidelity to
   the pre-registered trigger, not hindsight P&L.
4. **Wins that mattered were sized to caps, not vibes:** Chrysler to the 5% regulatory max, AmEx
   to the written 40% ceiling, KO to ~37%, Tepper's book concentrated into financials. The same
   behavior without the pre-set cap is the loss column (Valeant, Sequoia, Miller). The cap *is*
   the difference between conviction and catastrophe.
5. **Both sides of a famous trade can be right:** Icahn made ~$1.3bn on the exact position where
   Ackman lost ~$1bn — different structures (long equity + board seats vs naked short then
   puts), different horizons, different carry. Trade structure is not a detail; it decides who
   survives to be right.
6. **P&L opacity is the norm, not the exception:** Herbalife's exact loss, BYD's total, Burry's
   GME profit, Baupost's Lehman P&L — all U. Any Phase 12 system that benchmarks itself against
   "famous trade returns" is benchmarking against journalism.
