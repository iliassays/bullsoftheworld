# Phase 2 — Strategy-Family Taxonomy

**Date:** 2026-07-19. Evidence labels as in Phase 1. Win-rate figures are given ONLY where credible
published evidence exists; otherwise "U". Academic citations named inline; formal source list
consolidated in the Phase 16 appendix.

Edge types used throughout: **Structural** (rules/flows force others to trade), **Behavioral**
(persistent investor biases), **Informational** (better/faster data), **Analytical** (better
processing of shared data), **Execution** (lower cost/impact than competitors).

## Family details

### 1. Long-only fundamental
**Edge:** deeper company work + longer horizon than the marginal (quarter-driven) holder — "time
arbitrage." Behavioral/analytical. **Ideas from:** screens, industry coverage, management meetings,
supply-chain checks. **Data:** filings, transcripts, sell-side estimates, industry data — all
retail-accessible except management access. **Win rate:** U as a family; slugging matters more —
in diversified funds a few large winners drive most alpha (Bessembinder 2018: 4% of stocks account
for all net wealth creation above T-bills — V, academic). **Sizing:** conviction-weighted, 1–5%
positions, sector bands vs benchmark. **Bad regimes:** momentum melt-ups, narrow mega-cap markets
(2023's Magnificent-7 problem for active managers). **Capacity:** enormous. **Decay:** SPIVA
scorecards (V): most active large-cap funds lag the S&P over 10–15 yrs net of fees — the family
works better the further you get from mega-cap coverage saturation. **Retail:** fully replicable
mechanically; the institutional advantages (management access, analyst armies) matter least in
small/under-covered names.

### 2. Concentrated value
**Edge:** willingness to look wrong for years; buying below conservative intrinsic value.
Behavioral (career risk keeps institutions from doing it). **Ideas:** reading + inversion + few
decisions/yr (Buffett's "20-slot punch card" — MS). **Holding:** 3–10+ yr, 5–20 positions, single
positions to 25–40% (Berkshire/AAPL ~40%+ of equity book at peak — V from 13F). **Win rate:** U;
the model is low-frequency, high-slugging. **Risks:** single-thesis error is fatal without balance-
sheet strength; Sequoia/Valeant is the audited counterexample (V). **Regimes:** underperforms long
growth/momentum regimes (Berkshire lagged badly 1998–99 — V). **Retail:** replicable in mechanics;
the binding constraint is temperament and analytical honesty, not access. **Decay:** low — the
edge is a time preference most capital cannot hold.

### 3. Quality
**Edge:** market persistently underprices durable profitability/low accruals/conservative
investment. Behavioral. **Evidence:** Asness–Frazzini–Pedersen "Quality Minus Junk" (2019, V
academic): quality factor positive across 24 markets. Novy-Marx (2013) gross profitability.
**Data:** fundamentals only — fully retail-accessible. **Sizing:** typically a tilt/screen layered
on value or an index. **Risks:** quality gets expensive; paying any price for quality is a growth
trap. **Regimes:** shines in downturns/flights to safety; lags junk rallies (2009, 2020 recovery
legs). **Retail:** yes, cheaply. **Decay:** moderate — well-published, but the behavioral root
(lottery preference for junk) persists.

### 4. Growth
**Edge claimed:** earning power compounds longer than the market discounts (duration mispricing).
**Reality check:** academically, high growth alone has NO return premium — the investment/issuance
factors go the other way (Fama–French 2015 — V academic). The Tiger-cub record shows the family's
true shape: huge beta-plus-crowding upside (2020) then -50%+ when rates repriced (2022 — V/SI,
Phase 1). **Win rate:** U. **Retail:** buying growth is easy; the institutional version's real
inputs (private-market cross-ownership, expert networks) are not. **Verdict:** treat as a regime
exposure, not an edge, absent stock-specific variant perception.

### 5. GARP
**Edge:** growth not yet priced — Lynch's "buy what the market hasn't noticed compounding at
reasonable P/E," PEG heuristic (MS, Lynch's books). Magellan ~29%/yr 1977–90 (V, audited) — but
with 100%+ turnover, 1,000+ holdings at times, and a capacity/burnout ending. **Risks:** growth
estimates are the failure point; a GARP stock that stops growing is just an expensive cyclical.
**Retail:** highly replicable — Lynch's own thesis was that retail's ground-level observation is
an input advantage; treat that claim as MS and unproven at scale.

### 6. Long-short equity
**Edge:** alpha on both tails + reduced net exposure. **The documented catch:** the short book is
structurally expensive (borrow fees, squeezes, unlimited loss, market drift against you).
Greenlight 2015/2018 (MS letters) and Melvin 2021 (V) are the canonical cost exhibits. Aggregate
L/S index returns net of fees have lagged simple 60/40 for most of the post-2009 era (SI, HFR data
widely reported). **Sizing:** longs 2–5%, shorts 0.5–2% with hard stops or options-defined risk.
**Regimes:** works in dispersion-rich, rate-normal markets; bleeds in momentum melt-ups and squeeze
regimes. **Retail:** long side yes; short side realistically only via puts/defined-risk structures.

### 7. Equity market neutral
**Edge:** cross-sectional relative pricing with beta hedged out; returns from breadth (many small
independent bets — Grinold's fundamental law, V academic). **Requires:** factor models, borrow
infrastructure, tight costs. **Positions:** hundreds+; leverage 3–8× gross typical (SI). **Risks:**
factor crowding unwinds (Aug 2007 "quant quake" — V academic, Khandani–Lo 2011); leverage makes
small dislocations existential. **Retail:** effectively no — costs and leverage access.

### 8. Multi-factor systematic
**Edge:** harvesting published, persistent cross-sectional premia (value, momentum, quality,
low-vol) with disciplined implementation. **Evidence:** strongest academic base in this study
(Fama–French, Jegadeesh–Titman, AQR's own library — V academic) **and** the strongest documented
drought: AQR -38.7% 2018–2020 then record recovery (Phase 1, SI/V). **Implementation is the edge:**
DFA's audited funds show the premia are capturable net of costs (V); the graveyard of factor ETFs
shows sloppy implementation isn't. **Retail:** yes — long-only factor tilts via cheap funds, or
direct rules-based portfolios at modest scale. **Decay:** post-publication premium decay ~30–50%
(McLean–Pontiff 2016 — V academic) but not to zero for the big four.

### 9. Statistical arbitrage
**Edge:** thousands of small, fleeting cross-sectional mispricings; profits from breadth ×
turnover × execution quality. Medallion: ~66% gross with ~50.75%-ish win rates on millions of
trades is the folklore (Zuckerman quotes "right 50.75% of the time" — MS via book). Everything
about signals is **U/proprietary**. **Requires:** clean tick data, low-latency execution,
borrow, co-location. **Retail:** no — the cost stack IS the moat. RIEF's mediocrity (SI) shows
even Renaissance can't export it to slower, bigger capital.

### 10. Momentum
**Edge:** behavioral under-reaction/herding; 3–12-month relative strength persists. **Evidence:**
Jegadeesh–Titman 1993 + three decades out-of-sample, everywhere (V academic). **The catch:**
momentum crashes — 2009 junk rally cost the factor ~-70%+ on the short side (Daniel–Moskowitz
2016 — V academic). **Turnover:** high; the premium survives realistic costs only with patient
implementation (Frazzini–Israel–Moskowitz 2012 — V academic, AQR's own trading data). **Retail:**
long-only momentum tilts, monthly rebalance, yes.

### 11. Short-term mean reversion
**Edge:** liquidity provision — being paid to absorb imbalances (structural). Strong gross returns
in daily/weekly reversal (Lehmann 1990 — V academic) that are mostly a bid-ask/impact illusion at
retail cost levels. **Retail:** no as a standalone; useful only as an execution-timing overlay
(buy weakness when you were buying anyway).

### 12. Event-driven
**Edge:** forced/mechanical flows and complexity aversion around corporate events (spin-offs,
splits, index changes, post-bankruptcy equities). **Evidence:** spin-off excess returns documented
(Cusatis et al. 1993 — V academic; decayed since). Third Point's record (Phase 1) is the
practitioner exhibit. **Requires:** reading documents nobody reads — an effort edge, genuinely
retail-available in small caps. **Risks:** event breaks; deal-flow droughts.

### 13. Merger arbitrage
**Edge:** insurance premium for bearing deal-break risk (structural risk transfer).
**Evidence:** Mitchell–Pulvino 2001 (V academic, 4,750 deals 1963–98): ~4pp/yr over risk-free
pre-cost, with nonlinear (short-put-like) payoff — win often, lose big on breaks. **Win rate:**
high (most deals close) — the textbook case of why win rate ≠ profitability; expectancy lives in
sizing the breaks. **Retail:** liquid announced large-cap deals only; spreads are thin post-2010
(crowding), and leverage is what makes institutional returns — treat as a low-return diversifier.

### 14. Activist
**Edge:** control rights + credibility force the catalyst instead of waiting for it.
**Evidence:** Brav–Jiang–Partnoy–Thomas 2008 (V academic): ~7% abnormal return around 13D filings;
long-term operating improvements contested but positive in follow-ups. Elliott/PSH/Third Point
records (Phase 1). **Requires:** stake size, legal war-chest, reputation — **not replicable**;
the retail-adjacent version is *following* 13D filings (Phase 8 covers the workflow and its decay).

### 15. Distressed / restructuring
**Edge:** informational (few can model a capital structure in bankruptcy) + structural (forced
sellers: mandates prohibit holding defaulted paper). **Cycle-dependent:** feast 2002/2009/2020,
famine between. **Retail:** no — access to claims, legal process, and lot sizes.

### 16. Special situations
**Edge:** complexity + neglect in spin-offs, post-bankruptcy equities, thin-float situations.
Greenblatt's *You Can Be a Stock Market Genius* (MS + partial academic support) is the playbook;
his stated 50%/yr Gotham record 1985–94 is **MS, unaudited — do not treat as fact.** **Retail:**
the ONE family where small size is an outright advantage (institutions can't buy $200M spin-offs).
**Decay:** real — spin-off premium has compressed since popularization (SI).

### 17. Global macro (equity-relevant)
**Edge claimed:** regime analysis across rates/FX/equities. Bridgewater's ~11.4% (MS) and the
closed Druckenmiller record (MS, unverifiable) are the exhibits; public evidence of *persistent*
macro alpha net of fees is weak. **Retail:** directional macro expression is easy (futures/ETFs);
the edge is not. Use for risk framing (what regime am I in?), not as a return engine.

### 18. Volatility / options
**Edge:** variance risk premium — implied vol persistently exceeds realized (V academic,
Carr–Wu 2009 et al.). Short-vol harvests it and periodically explodes (Feb 2018 XIV termination,
-96% in a day — V). **Retail:** only defined-risk structures (spreads, collars, covered calls);
never unhedged short gamma. Institutional market-makers hold this edge structurally via flow and
hedging infrastructure you cannot copy.

### 19. Multi-manager platform
**Not a strategy — an organizational technology:** many independent pods on tight risk budgets
(typical public descriptions: pods run ~$50–100M+ notional per PM, stopped out at -5% to -7.5%,
firm-level VaR + factor caps, pass-through costs — **SI, press-consistent, no primary doc**).
Edge = diversification across alphas + ruthless capital reallocation + central execution/financing.
Millennium's one losing year in 35 (Phase 1) is the result. **Retail:** the model is not copyable,
but its *risk grammar* (per-strategy drawdown stops, volatility budgeting, forced de-risking) is —
that observation feeds Systems B/C and the Phase 15 rulebook.

### 20. Multi-strategy (single book)
D.E. Shaw/Baupost style: one balance sheet, opportunistic allocation across families. Edge =
capital flexibility ("go where the stress is"). Requires breadth of team; retail analog is
regime-aware allocation across your own few validated strategies — feeds Phase 12.

### 21. Index arbitrage
Historical structural edge (futures-cash basis, index-inclusion front-running). Now compressed to
market-maker infrastructure costs. Retail-relevant residue: **avoid being the counterparty** —
don't trade mechanically around known index events. The index-inclusion effect itself has decayed
toward zero post-2010 (V academic, multiple studies).

### 22. Market making / HFT
Execution/structural edge: spread capture + speed. Virtu's IPO prospectus (V, S-1): one losing
day in 1,238 trading days. Pure infrastructure moat. **Retail:** zero replicability; the lesson
is cost-side only — you pay this industry on every trade, so trade less and use limits.

## Cross-family findings (feed Phases 11–12)

1. **The retail-replicable set is small and specific:** long-only fundamental (esp. small/neglected),
   concentrated value, quality/value/momentum factor tilts, special situations/spin-offs, GARP,
   defined-risk options overlays, and filings-following (with decay discipline). Everything else
   is either infrastructure-gated or cost-gated.
2. **Win rate and edge are unrelated across families.** Merger arb: high win rate, short-put payoff.
   Stat-arb: ~51% win rate, extreme profitability via breadth. Concentrated value: low decision
   count, slugging-driven. Any system design must specify expectancy, not accuracy (Phase 13
   makes this a required metric).
3. **Every family has a documented regime in which it loses for years.** Value 2017–2020, momentum
   2009, growth 2022, macro 2010s, quality in junk rallies. Strategy suspension rules and multi-year
   patience are not optional features — they are the difference between AQR (survived to +43.5% in
   2022) and the funds that liquidated at the 2020 trough.
4. **The strongest evidence supports the most boring conclusions:** factor premia exist but decay
   ~30–50% post-publication; most active funds lag after fees; the audited outperformers won by
   +0.2–2pp/yr, not +10pp/yr. Any system promising more than low-single-digit annual edge over its
   benchmark should be presumed overfit until proven otherwise — this becomes a red-team rule in
   Phase 16.
