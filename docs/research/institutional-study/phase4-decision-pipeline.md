# Phase 4 — The Institutional Decision Pipeline

**Date:** 2026-07-19. This phase reconstructs the 25-step workflow across the selected case
studies. Honesty note: **no institution publishes its pipeline end-to-end.** What follows is
assembled from documented fragments (letters, annual reports, court/regulatory records, credible
books) with per-step evidence labels. Steps with no credible public documentation are marked U
rather than invented — and the *pattern of what is documented vs U* is itself a finding: inputs
and theses get published; sizing math and risk limits almost never do.

## 4.1 The 25 steps, with documented evidence

**Stage I — Universe & sourcing (steps 1–4)**

1. **Universe definition.** Best documented at the systematic shops: DFA/AQR publish eligibility
   rules (liquidity, market-cap, exchange listing — V, prospectuses). Fundamental shops declare
   qualitative universes: Buffett's circle of competence (V, letters), TCI's moat-infrastructure
   universe (MS). Platform pods are assigned sub-universes by mandate (SI, consistent press).
   *Every* documented process starts by excluding most of the market.
2. **Idea generation.** Phase 3. Note the documented asymmetry: fundamental shops generate few
   ideas and kill most; quant shops "generate" the whole universe and rank it.
3. **Initial screening.** Fundamental: disqualification checklists (leverage, accounting flags,
   governance) — MS across letters; Baupost's stated first question is "what can we lose?" (MS,
   Klarman's *Margin of Safety*). Quant: factor eligibility + tradability filters (V,
   prospectuses).
4. **Analyst assignment.** Documented only structurally: sector pods (platforms — SI),
   generalists (Berkshire: none; Buffett + Munger read everything — V by omission), central
   research + PM sponsor (Third Point, Elliott — SI from staffing descriptions). No evidence any
   assignment model outperforms; what recurs is *sponsorship* — a named person owns the thesis.

**Stage II — Research & thesis (steps 5–11)**

5. **Primary research.** Documented range: Pershing Square's public research decks (V — they
   publish the actual work: Herbalife ~340 slides, MBIA, the Valeant defense) are the best public
   window into institutional depth-per-name. Elliott's distressed work is legal-first (court
   filings in Argentina/Lehman cases — V). Scuttlebutt-style channel checks: MS everywhere,
   verified nowhere.
6. **Financial modeling.** Universally claimed, never published in full. The documented *shape*:
   scenario ranges rather than point targets (PSH ARs present per-position bull/base/bear
   framing — V that they do it). U on mechanics everywhere.
7. **Variant perception.** The load-bearing concept, from Steinhardt (V, his memoir names it):
   a specific, articulable disagreement with consensus plus a reason the market will converge.
   Documented positive example: Greenlight's Lehman short — the variant perception (level-3 asset
   marks) was stated publicly in May 2008 before the collapse (V, speech transcript). Documented
   *absence*: Tiger 2022 — long duration-growth at peak multiples was the *consensus* of the
   crowd that shared the position (Phase 1). **Working rule: no variant perception, no position —
   only beta.**
8. **Catalyst analysis.** Event shops require one (Third Point letters — MS); deep value often
   proceeds without (Baupost — MS) accepting longer convergence. Activists *manufacture* the
   catalyst (13D → campaign → board seats: V, filings). The documented failure mode is
   catalyst-free value that stays cheap ("value trap") — the 2017–20 factor drought at stock level.
9. **Bull/base/bear scenarios.** Documented at PSH (V, ARs), stated by most (MS). The
   institutional point is not the three numbers but the *pre-commitment*: the bear case is written
   down before entry, so later price action can be compared to a prior, not to a rationalization.
10. **Expected-return calculation.** U in detail everywhere private. Only systematic shops have
    verifiable versions (factor-model expected returns — V methodologically via published research).
11. **Downside estimation.** The step the failures skipped, per the records: Amaranth's risk
    reports showed scenario losses that leadership overrode (V, Senate PSI report); Archegos ran
    no external risk process at all (V, court records); Sequoia's board dissent on Valeant sizing
    was overruled and the dissenters resigned (V, reported board minutes/press). The survivors'
    common documented trait: a written answer to "what if we're wrong?" before entry.

**Stage III — Portfolio fit (steps 12–15)**

12. **Liquidity analysis.** Documented as *days-to-exit at participation caps* thinking (SI from
    platform risk descriptions; V at NBIM which publishes liquidity frameworks). Failures:
    Archegos held >10% of float in names via swaps — exit was mechanically impossible (V).
13. **Crowding analysis.** Modern platforms screen prime-broker crowding data (SI); Melvin is the
    documented cost of skipping it — >100% short interest in GME was public data (V) before
    January 2021.
14. **Correlation/factor analysis.** Systematic: native. Fundamental: the documented failure is
    thematic correlation invisible to sector labels (Tiger's "different" longs were one rates
    bet — Phase 1). Platforms cap factor exposure per pod (SI, consistent descriptions).
15. **Position-size decision.** Phase 5's subject. Documented range: Kelly-aware discretion
    (Buffett/Munger statements — MS), risk-parity-ish vol budgeting (systematic — V
    methodologically), platform notional caps with stop-outs (SI). U on exact math at every
    private fundamental shop.

**Stage IV — Approval & entry (steps 16–19)**

16. **PM approval.** Universal chokepoint; documented only structurally. Single-PM shops: the PM
    is the risk system (key-person risk — Phase 6). Platforms: pod PM sizes within centrally
    imposed limits (SI).
17. **Investment committee.** Documented at allocators (Yale/NBIM — V, governance documents) and
    activists pre-campaign (SI). Notably ABSENT by design at Berkshire (V — Buffett: committees
    don't beat markets) — governance heterogeneity is real; what recurs is *someone* with veto
    power independent of the idea's author, except where the founder IS the fund (the key-person
    concentration the failures share).
18. **Entry planning.** Phase 7. Documented: activists accumulate under 13D thresholds using
    swaps/options within the 10-day (now 5-day — V, 2023 SEC rule change) disclosure window.
19. **Execution.** Phase 7. Institutional TCA/algo infrastructure documented extensively in
    market-structure literature (V).

**Stage V — Life of the position (steps 20–25)**

20. **Monitoring.** Documented artifacts: thesis dashboards/KPI tracking (MS across letters);
    quarterly re-underwriting (PSH ARs re-state each thesis annually — V that they publish it).
21. **Thesis updates.** The documented discipline is *asymmetric*: price moves alone don't update
    the thesis; new fundamental information does. Phase 6's averaging-down investigation lives
    exactly here.
22. **Add/reduce rules.** U in numeric form at fundamental shops; structural at platforms
    (drawdown de-risking is mechanical — SI, multiple consistent descriptions of ~-5%/-7.5%
    pod stop-outs, never primary-verified).
23. **Hedging.** Documented spectacular case: PSH's Feb 2020 CDS hedge — $27M premium into $2.6bn
    proceeds (V, PSH 2020 AR) — bought when spreads were at historic tights, i.e., cheap convexity
    against a specific identified risk, not a standing program. Standing tail programs: rarely
    documented as economic (SI, industry lit.).
24. **Exit.** Phase 7. Pre-registered invalidation is the documented differentiator (Greenlight's
    stated practice of exiting when the thesis is disproven even at a loss — MS letters).
25. **Post-investment review.** Documented publicly at PSH (annual report post-mortems on Valeant,
    Netflix — V), Bridgewater (error-log culture — MS, *Principles*), Greenlight (annual letter
    loss reviews — MS but public and specific). The failures have no documented internal review
    culture; several (Archegos) had no external reporting at all.

## 4.2 The nine questions (with which institution documents each)

| Question | Best documented exemplar |
|---|---|
| What must be true for this to work? | PSH public theses (V) |
| What do we believe that the market doesn't? | Steinhardt's variant perception (V, memoir); Greenlight Lehman (V) |
| Why hasn't the market corrected it? | Forced-seller/neglect logic (Phase 3 §3.2); Baupost's stated sourcing from "urgent sellers" (MS) |
| What makes the market recognize it? | Activist campaigns literally supply it (V, 13Ds) |
| What evidence invalidates the thesis? | Greenlight exit discipline (MS); the *absence* is the Valeant/Sequoia record (V) |
| Maximum loss if wrong? | Klarman's first-question framing (MS); Amaranth as the documented negative (V, Senate) |
| Is the return worth the risk? | Scenario-weighted expected value framing (PSH — V format, MS numbers) |
| How does it interact with the book? | Platform factor caps (SI); Tiger 2022 as negative (SI) |
| What would make us exit before target? | Pre-registered invalidation (MS across disciplined shops); U numerically |

## 4.3 Three pipeline archetypes (for Phase 12 system design)

- **Concentrated fundamental (Berkshire/TCI/PSH):** few decisions, months of research per name,
  approval = principal conviction, monitoring = business KPIs, exit = thesis/valuation. The entire
  pipeline optimizes *decision quality per decision*. Replicable in shape at any scale.
- **Platform pod (Millennium/Citadel):** many small time-boxed theses, sizing/risk imposed
  externally and mechanically, exit dominated by risk rules not thesis rules. Optimizes *loss
  control across many mediocre-to-good decisions*. The mechanical risk grammar is the replicable
  part (Phase 15); the diversification across 300 pods is not.
- **Systematic (AQR/DFA/Renaissance):** the pipeline IS software: universe → signals → optimizer →
  execution, with humans supervising process integrity, not picks. Fully replicable in *structure*
  at small scale; the Two Sigma enforcement case (V, Phase 1) documents the governance failure
  mode unique to this archetype — unsupervised model change.

**Cross-archetype invariant (the Phase 11 headline candidate):** in every documented survivor, the
downside is examined before the upside and by someone/something other than the idea's author. In
every documented failure, that separation was absent or overridden.
