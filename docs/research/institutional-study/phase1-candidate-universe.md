# Phase 1 — Candidate Universe & Screening

**Date researched:** 2026-07-19. All "current" figures are as of their cited publication dates, not
live. Evidence categories: **V** = Verified (primary/audited/regulatory), **MS** = Manager-stated,
**SI** = Strongly inferred, **WI** = Weakly inferred, **U** = Unknown/proprietary.

## A note on the evidence landscape (read first)

Private hedge-fund returns are almost never independently auditable by the public. What exists:

1. **Audited, public NAVs** — listed vehicles (Pershing Square Holdings), mutual funds (AQR, DFA,
   Fidelity), sovereign funds (NBIM), endowment reports (Yale). Score 5 possible.
2. **Rating-agency / prospectus disclosures** — Citadel and Millennium disclose some fund data to
   bond investors (KBRA-rated notes). Score 3–4.
3. **Investor letters quoted in credible press** — Elliott, Greenlight, Third Point, TCI. The letter
   numbers are manager-stated; press consistency across years upgrades them to SI at best.
4. **Books with primary access** — Zuckerman on Renaissance. Secondary but heavily sourced.
5. **Court/regulatory records for failures** — ironically the *failures* (Archegos, LTCM, Amaranth,
   Two Sigma's model-tampering) have the best evidence quality of anything in this study.

Consequence: this study treats **failure cases as the highest-evidence data** and treats headline
hedge-fund CAGRs as manager-stated unless a listed/audited vehicle exists.

## Screening observations (feed Phase 11)

1. **The audited tier clusters at lower returns.** NBIM +0.24pp/yr relative; Contrafund ~+1-2pp/yr;
   Berkshire's edge has shrunk toward the index since ~2003. The 15–20%+ CAGRs live almost entirely
   in the self-reported tier. That is not proof they're false — but the correlation between
   verifiability and modesty is itself a finding.
2. **Every long-record institution has at least one catastrophe or long drought** (Berkshire -50%
   twice; Citadel -55%; AQR -39%; PSH three losing years; TCI -43%; Yale -24.6%). Survival +
   process reform after the catastrophe, not catastrophe avoidance, is the shared trait.
3. **Platform funds (Millennium, Citadel, D.E. Shaw) have the smoothest return paths** — and the
   least replicable model (thousands of employees, centralized risk, pass-through fees).
4. **The failures share a signature:** concentration + leverage + liquidity mismatch + crowding,
   with governance unable to stop the trade (Amaranth's trader had veto-proof P&L status;
   Sequoia's board dissenters resigned; Archegos had no external governance at all).
