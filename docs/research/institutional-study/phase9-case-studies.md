# Phase 9 — Detailed Case Studies (14+ Institutions)

**Date:** 2026-07-19. Labels V/MS/SI/WI/U per Phase 1. Each case: structure → process artifacts →
defining episode → worst episode & response → succession → what's replicable. Several Phase 1–6
ledger IOUs are settled below (Third Point 2008, PSH 2015–17, Buffett/AmEx 40%, RenTec leverage);
corrections are folded into `ledger.md`. Two cross-cutting findings up front:

1. **The survivors manage the denominator as actively as the numerator.** Documented across the
   whole platform/quant tier and beyond: Citadel has returned **$32bn since 2017** (SI, CNBC);
   D.E. Shaw returned ~half of 2024 profits and has kept Composite closed since 2013 (SI);
   Millennium returned $15bn in 2022 while forcing remaining capital into 5-year terms (SI);
   Medallion is capped ~$10bn with profits swept out (SI); Bridgewater shrank Pure Alpha toward a
   $50–60bn target *on purpose* (SI); Greenlight re-closed at ~$2–3bn in 2026 (SI); Elliott raises
   in discrete windows. Capacity discipline is as documented as any alpha claim in this study.
2. **Succession divides the tier cleanly** — solved structurally (D.E. Shaw's executive committee
   has run the firm for 20+ years post-founder; Bridgewater completed a signed control transfer;
   Berkshire's Abel became CEO Jan 1, 2026 — V, 8-K; Contrafund added comanagers with a dated
   retirement plan — V, SEC 497; Dimensional runs on an investment committee) versus unsolved and
   founder-keyed (TCI, Greenlight, Third Point, Citadel). The unsolved list overlaps heavily with
   the concentrated-conviction style — the style hardest to hand over.

## 9.1 Concentrated fundamental & activist

### Berkshire Hathaway (evidence 5)

- **Structure:** insurance float **≈$176bn at YE2025** (V, 2025 AR) — permanent negative-cost
  leverage no fund structure can copy; cash+T-bills **≈$369bn at YE2025** (V — note: the widely
  cited "record $325bn" was the YE2024 figure; it kept growing).
- **Process artifact:** the published acquisition-criteria ad (V, decades of ARs): consistent
  earning power ("future projections are of no interest to us"), good ROE with little debt,
  management in place, simple businesses, price stated upfront. The most public
  disqualification-first checklist in existence (Phase 4 step 3's exemplar).
- **Defining trade (modern):** Japan trading houses — bought from July 2019, ~10% of each of the
  five sogo shosha; **cost $15.4bn, market value $35.4bn at YE2025** (V, AR/letters); financed
  with **~¥1.3tn of yen bonds at ~1.2% average coupon** roughly matching the yen cost basis
  (V/MS, 2024 letter) — a currency-hedged carry structure. The entry took *years* and the
  disclosure was staged (Phase 7's accumulation pattern at maximum scale).
- **Worst episode:** Dexter Shoe 1993 — paid in stock, went to zero (MS, letters); no formal
  process doc changed, but Berkshire's aversion to stock-as-currency after it is observable.
- **Succession (resolved, V):** board vote May 2025; **Abel CEO from Jan 1, 2026**; Buffett stays
  chairman; bylaws amended Sept 2025 to separate the roles. Fifty years of planning, executed.
- **Replicable:** the checklist, the write-the-price-first discipline, the patience. Not the
  float, and not the 40%-position tolerance without the permanent capital behind it.

### Pershing Square Holdings (evidence 5)

- **Structure:** listed closed-end (permanent capital since Oct 2014); 1.5% mgmt + 16%
  performance with HWM, performance fee reduced by 20% of fees from other PS vehicles (V, AIC/
  filings). ~11 positions, top-10 = 99.3% of the book (V, 13F).
- **Verified Valeant-era arc (V, PSH 2023 AR performance table — closes the Phase 1 IOU):**
  2015 **-20.5%**, 2016 **-13.5%**, 2017 **-4.0%**; then 2019 +58.1%, 2020 +70.2%, 2021 +26.9%,
  2022 -8.8%, 2023 +26.7%, 2024 +10.2%, 2025 +20.9% — permanent-capital-era CAGR 22.2% (V, 2025
  AR/2026 presentation). The same manager, the same concentration — bracketed by a written
  process reform.
- **The reform artifact (MS):** eight principles **physically engraved on stone tablets on every
  desk** after Valeant — simple/predictable/FCF-generative, barriers to entry, high ROIC, limited
  extrinsic risk, balance sheets that don't need capital-market access, governance. Ackman's own
  claim: every significant loss traced to violating ≥1 principle; Valeant violated the
  capital-markets-dependence rule *knowingly*. Also stated: no more public short campaigns
  (post-Herbalife 2018).
- **CDS trade fully verified this pass (V, PSCM letter of Mar 26, 2020):** ~$27M premiums+
  commissions → **$2.6bn proceeds** (of which $2.1bn to PSH), exit completed the morning of
  Mar 23, 2020, ~$2.5bn redeployed into equities at the lows. The Phase 6 "specific risk bought
  cheaply" exemplar now sits on its primary source.
- **Capital-raising record is mixed and instructive (V):** PSUS IPO withdrawn July 2024
  ($25bn target → ~$2bn) — then a redesigned $5bn PSUS IPO completed April 2026. Even the best
  permanent-capital engineer got the first ask badly wrong.
- **Succession:** Ryan Israel named CIO 2022 (SI) — the explicit key-person answer; Ackman
  remains the public face.
- **Replicable:** the engraved-principles mechanism (a pre-commitment device, not a poster), the
  scenario-per-position AR format, permanent capital as the enabler of concentration.

### TCI (evidence 3.5)

- **Structure:** founded 2003; fee innovation — 0.5pp of the management fee pledged to the
  Children's Investment Fund Foundation at launch (SI); AUM ~$19bn (2007) → **<$5bn after 2008**
  → **~$77bn (2025–26)** with a record **$18.9bn profit in 2025** (SI, Forbes/II). ~10–15
  mega-cap "moat" holdings, multi-year, visibly low 13F turnover (SI).
- **Defining campaigns (V):** Deutsche Börse 2005 (killed the LSE bid, ousted the CEO);
  ABN AMRO 2007 (~1% stake forced the breakup — a "win" that helped detonate RBS); **CSX 2008 —
  the cautionary one: SDNY held TCI violated §13(d) via undisclosed cash-settled swaps** (V,
  court record — the same instrument-disclosure gap Phases 7–8 track); CN/KCS 2021 (opposed CN's
  bid while holding both CN and CP; CP won KCS); Safran/Zodiac 2017 (bid repriced lower, in cash).
- **Worst episode & response:** 2008 -43% (SI), AUM collapse, CSX humiliation → pivoted from
  hostile activism to concentrated quality-compounder "engaged" investing. The style everyone
  now associates with TCI is the *post-failure* style.
- **Succession: U — the least-documented of the tier.** Hohn personally takes the concentration
  decisions; nothing disclosed.
- **Replicable:** the reformed style (10–15 quality infrastructure-like compounders, held for
  years) is the most retail-shaped portfolio in the study; the activism that seeds it is not.

### Elliott Management (evidence 3.5)

- **Structure:** founded 1977; ~$79.8bn AUM (V, firm site); multi-strategy with drift toward
  locked capital (raising a $7bn drawdown vehicle, 2025–26 — SI). **Only two down years since
  1977: 1998 (~-7%) and 2008 (~-3%)** (SI — Fortune 2012, widely corroborated, never audited
  publicly; magnitudes now on the ledger with that label).
- **Defining trade (Argentina/NML):** defaulted bonds bought ~20–25c post-2001; the pari passu
  injunction (2012); settled Feb 2016 within a **$4.65bn** payment ratified by Argentine law (V);
  profit ≈$2.4bn/10–15x is journalistic reconstruction (SI — Elliott never published cost basis).
  Fifteen years of capital lockup and court risk — the stress case was the *duration*, not a
  drawdown.
- **Documented activist pattern (SI):** quiet accumulation (often partly derivatives) → private
  letter → public letter + white paper → full-slate threat → settle for seats. Positions $1–5bn
  but *small in %* (TXN ~1.3%; Honeywell $5bn+). Recent record: Southwest 2024 (5 directors
  seated — V), Honeywell (3-way breakup announced Feb 2025 — V; causal credit contested, as with
  all activism — attribution inflation flagged).
- **Succession:** already operating — co-CEOs Pollock and Gordon Singer run day-to-day with Paul
  Singer (81) as founder/co-CEO (SI).
- **Replicable:** the 13D event stream around campaigns like these is Phase 8's headline signal;
  the campaigns themselves obviously aren't.

### Third Point (evidence 3)

- **Structure:** flagship offshore + LSE-listed feeder (TPOU); 2019 restructuring cut fees
  2.0% → 1.25% with a 25% quarterly redemption gate (SI, filings/QuotedData); 2022–23 redemption
  wave paid out in installments (SI).
- **2008 settled (V — manager's own published performance sheet):** **Offshore fund -32.7%**;
  Partners LP -32.8%; Ultra (levered) -38.6%; then +38.6% in 2009. The ledger's "do not state a
  number" is now replaced with vehicle-labeled numbers.
- **Defining trade (Yahoo 2011–13, V via filings):** 5.1% stake at ~$13.02 avg with a public
  letter; exposed the CEO's résumé fabrication; board seats May 2012; hired Mayer; sold 40M
  shares back to Yahoo at $29.11 July 2013 (~$1.16bn). The full activist lifecycle —
  entry-letter-board-catalyst-exit — in one documented case.
- **Style drift is the honest headline (SI):** poison-pen activism → constructive mega-cap
  (Nestlé $3.5bn, 2017) → growth/venture tilt into 2021 → **2022 -21.8%** → back to event-driven.
  2018 -11% (V-adjacent, letters). The drift correlates with every bad year — the Phase 6
  style-drift detector applied to a named fund.
- **Succession: U.** Loeb remains CEO/CIO; senior turnover through the 2010s–20s.
- **Replicable:** the caution. Third Point is the study's cleanest documented case that *style
  drift, not stock selection*, drives a talented fund's worst outcomes.

### Greenlight (evidence 3)

- **Structure:** founded 1996 (~$900k); ~$12bn peak (2014) → ~$1–1.5bn trough zone (2019–22, WI
  on exact trough — 13F ≠ AUM) → ~$2–2.9bn; reopened May 2020, **re-closed Jan 2026** at a size
  that "supports the business" (SI/II) — deliberate smallness as strategy.
- **Defining trade 1 (Allied Capital, V/MS):** May 2002 Sohn speech → six-year war including
  illegal pretexting of Einhorn's phone records and an SEC investigation *of Einhorn* →
  June 2007 SEC found Allied violated securities laws on valuation. The canonical documented
  case that being right on a short can take 5+ years and personal cost — Phase 2's
  short-selling economics in one story.
- **Defining trade 2 (Lehman, V):** public variant perception on Level-3 marks stated May 2008,
  four months before the bankruptcy (already the Phase 4 exemplar; timeline confirmed:
  first warning Nov 2007 at ~$64).
- **Worst episode:** 2015 -20%, **2018 -34%** (SI; -34 vs -38 split across sources is
  class/gross-net ambiguity — preserved), AUM collapse. **Stated adaptation (MS, specific and
  falsifiable):** stop buying 10–11x earnings hoping for re-rating; buy 4–5x with 15–20%
  cash-flow yields "where the company pays you" (buybacks/dividends), plus macro overlays.
  Results since: 2022 +36.6%, 2023 +22.1%, 2025 +9% (SI).
- **Succession: U** — singularly Einhorn-keyed.
- **Replicable:** the adaptation itself is the retail-relevant artifact: in a passive-flow
  market, the return path must come from the company's own capital returns, not from other
  buyers showing up. That thesis is testable in Phase 13.

## 9.2 Systematic & platform

### Millennium (evidence 2.5–3 on internals)

- **Structure/fees:** ~$79–83bn AUM (early 2026; figures conflict, floor taken); 2023 fee floor:
  **greater of ~1% or 20% of gains, plus full pass-through** — fees even in losing years (SI,
  Bloomberg). Five-year committed share classes since 2019; $15bn returned Oct 2022 to force the
  conversion (SI). >330 pods (vs ~280 per one source — unresolved), ~15–20%/yr PM turnover (WI).
- **The pod-rule hunt came back empty — status upgraded to a documented absence:** a dedicated
  search of court dockets (Jane Street v. Millennium settled before discovery surfaced anything),
  EDGAR, ADV filings found **no primary source** for the -5%/-7.5% rule. It stays SI-for-existence
  / WI-for-exact-numbers, now with the negative search on record. Ex-employee accounts add that
  real intervention starts at ~2.5–3% — the folklore may *overstate* the slack.
- **Succession = institutionalization-by-ownership (SI):** Nov 2025 sold 15% for ~$2bn at a
  ~$14bn firm valuation, explicitly framed as post-Englander preparation; no named heir. Note
  also the Jain Global round-trip (Apr 2026: returning external cash to run Millennium money
  exclusively — SI) — platform capital, not talent, is the scarce asset.
- **Replicable:** the drawdown-ladder *grammar* (Phase 15 backbone) — with the standing caveat
  that the exact numbers are folklore.

### Citadel (evidence 3)

- **Structure:** $90.4bn cumulative net gains since 1990 (SI — supersedes the stale ~$74bn
  figure); **$32bn returned since 2017**, starting 2026 at $67bn AUM (SI, CNBC); employees own
  ~$9bn (~20%) of Wellington (SI, Bloomberg). Citadel LLC and Citadel Securities formally
  separate since 2017 (V/SI); Securities' net trading revenue $9.7bn 2024, $12.2bn 2025 (SI).
- **Near-primary fee artifact (V-adjacent, bond prospectus via Hedgeweek):** three largest funds
  earned **$56.8bn gross 2021–Sep 2024; investors netted ~$30bn** after $7.5bn fees + **$17bn
  pass-through** (majority compensation). The pass-through economics in actual numbers — the
  best documentation of platform costs in the study.
- **Worst episode & response (SI):** 2008 -55% with gated redemptions; +62% in 2009; HWM crossed
  Jan 2012 (gate-lift timeline conflicts preserved). Griffin's stated lessons: term funding,
  documentation, longer-term investors (MS) — the returned-capital discipline is the structural
  descendant.
- **Succession: U.** Griffin remains CEO/majority owner of both entities; nothing announced —
  the largest unsolved key-person exposure in the platform tier.

### D.E. Shaw (evidence 3)

- **Structure:** >$85bn firm-wide (MS/SI; a $65bn alternatives-only figure also circulates —
  labeled); Composite **closed to new capital since mid-2013**; fees restored to **3/30** from
  2024 (SI, II); returned ~half of 2024 profits, then paused returns after a +18.5%/+28.2%
  (Composite/Oculus) 2025 (SI).
- **The governance finding:** founder withdrew from day-to-day management in the early 2000s;
  an **executive committee** has run the firm for 20+ years — the cleanest documented proof
  that a quant firm can institutionalize past its founder while he's still alive.
- **Replicable:** nothing operational (internals U), but the capacity discipline and fee-power
  sequence (close fund → compound record → raise fees) documents where platform economics
  actually come from: scarcity, not scale.

### Renaissance / Medallion (evidence 3, and U at fund level)

- **The internal/external divergence is the case study (SI, II):** 2020 — **Medallion +76%,
  RIEF -19.9%**. Same firm, same people, same infrastructure; short-horizon capacity-capped
  signals vs longer-horizon beta-heavy externals. The cleanest evidence in the study that
  **capacity and horizon, not intelligence, are the binding constraints.** RIEF's arc since:
  2024 +22.7% (best since 2011), then Oct 2025 -14.4% in one month (SI).
- **Senate PSI 2014 correction (V — primary):** basket-option leverage ran up to **20:1**
  (not the ~12.5x in circulation — ledger corrected); ~$34bn of profits through the structures;
  Senate-estimated tax avoided ~$6.8bn; the related IRS settlement (Sept 2021) was **up to
  ~$7bn** — two adjacent but distinct numbers, both now labeled. The PSI report also documents
  the banks' contractual loss-threshold/knock-out mechanics — **the only primary-documented
  drawdown-kill-switch grammar found anywhere in this study** (imported by Phase 15).
- **Medallion post-2024: U, full stop.** Simons died May 2024; all post-Simons continuity claims
  are secondary reconstruction; fees 5/44, capped ~$10bn, employees-only since 2005 (SI).
- **Replicable:** nothing about Medallion; everything about the honesty of the label.

### AQR (evidence 4 via funds)

- **The verified arc:** peak $226bn (2018) → ~$95–98bn (end-2022) → **$189bn firm-wide
  end-2025** (SI, Hedgeweek/AFI); layoffs two straight years through the winter (SI, Bloomberg);
  Absolute Return: 2021 +16.8%, **2022 +43.5% (best since 1998)**, 2023 +18.5% (SI, CNBC —
  extends the Phase 1 row).
- **Process artifact (V, published):** Asness's "The Less-Efficient Market Hypothesis" (2024,
  SSRN/JPM) — his formalized lesson from the quant winter: markets got *less* efficient at
  medium horizons (social media a named culprit), so the other side pays more but takes longer
  and hurts more. The published-research culture is itself the business strategy — the stated
  moat is implementation, not secrecy (MS — consistent with Phase 3's finding).
- **Replicable:** the factor exposures literally are (mutual funds/UCITS since 2009). What isn't:
  surviving the 2018–20 drawdown required a fee-paying institutional base and a firm that could
  lose 60% of AUM without dying. Phase 12 must size any factor sleeve to survive a three-year
  drought (the AQR row *is* the stress test).

### Dimensional (evidence 5)

- **Structure:** crossed **$1 trillion AUM Feb 2026** (MS/V, firm releases); largest active-ETF
  issuer; industry-largest mutual-fund→ETF conversions from Nov 2020; fees 9–25bps at the core
  (SI). No star PMs — investment committee + Nobel advisory bench; the process is the product.
- **The verified implementation edge (V, peer-reviewed — Keim JFE 1999):** DFA's small-cap fund
  beat its own target index by **+2.2%/yr (1982–1995)** through patient liquidity-*providing*
  execution and academic exclusion screens. The canonical proof that trading design alone can
  create a durable premium — but note the honest limit: no equally rigorous public update exists
  for the ETF era (U).
- **Replicable — uniquely, almost everything:** the ETFs directly (since 2020, no advisor gate);
  behaviorally, patience-as-liquidity-provision is precisely the retail advantage Phase 7 §7.5
  identified. DFA is the study's existence proof that the boring version works at scale.

### Bridgewater (evidence 2.5–3)

- **Structure:** ~$92bn (press basis) vs $136.5bn (regulatory discretionary incl. notional) —
  both true, denominators differ (SI); deliberate shrinkage toward a $50–60bn Pure Alpha target
  (SI); All Weather ETF (ALLW) with State Street since Mar 2025 (V) — the macro franchise
  productized at retail fees.
- **Performance honesty:** Pure Alpha ~4.2%/yr over the decade to ~2022 (SI); 2024 +11.3%;
  **2025 +33–34% — best year in firm history** (SI; class ambiguity preserved). One great year
  does not un-happen a weak decade; both go on the ledger.
- **Succession (V/SI — the completed founder exit):** signed control transfer to the operating
  board (2022), Bar Dea sole CEO (2023), **Dalio fully off the board (2025)** — with no
  redemption surge. The only fully executed founder→institution handover among the private funds.
- **Culture-as-process:** stated moat (MS) vs documented chronic early-tenure turnover (SI);
  whether the culture causes the alpha is U — labeled, not resolved.

## 9.3 Long-only & allocators

### Fidelity Magellan under Lynch (evidence 4, with corrections)

- **The record, properly bracketed:** 29.2%/yr 1977–90 (SI, universal but no fetchable audited
  primary) — **but the fund was closed to the public until mid-1981**; the publicly investable
  record is ~22.5% vs 16.5% (V-adjacent, Carlson/Bernstein analysis). Both true; they answer
  different questions. Turnover ~300%/yr early, ~110% later (SI).
- **The famous claim is apocryphal (finding of this pass):** "the average Magellan investor lost
  money" traces to a "Fidelity study" **no one has ever produced**. The documented version is
  weaker and more useful: **Lynch's own calculation that the average investor earned ~7%/yr
  against the fund's 29%** (MS via Jakab) — destroyed by timing, not by the fund. The ledger
  carries it as WI/apocryphal-as-stated.
- **Process artifacts (MS):** ~200 company visits + 700 annual reports/yr, six stock categories,
  PEG; the "1000+ visits" version is WI. Post-Lynch: Vinik's 1995 bond shift, Stansky's
  closet-index era (238% vs S&P 274% — V/SI), reopening Jan **2008** (not 2006 — corrected).
- **Replicable:** the categories, the PEG sanity check, and above all the dollar-weighted lesson:
  the gap between fund return and investor return is behavior, and it's the one variable retail
  fully controls.

### Fidelity Contrafund / Danoff (evidence 5)

- **Record (V, Morningstar):** **14.1%/yr Sept 1990–Jul 2025 vs 11.2% S&P — +2.9pp/yr over 35
  years** at $170bn+ scale; the largest solely-managed active equity fund in the world until
  comanagers were added Apr 2025.
- **Process artifacts (MS/SI):** ~5 management meetings/day captured in decades of longhand
  notebooks; "stock prices follow earnings"; best-of-breed within known industries. The
  uncomfortable modern finding (SI, Morningstar): a quality-factor ETF now nearly replicates the
  portfolio — the record is real; its forward replicability is contested and labeled.
- **Succession (V, SEC 497):** Anolic + Weiner comanagers Apr 2025, ~30% reallocated by Nov 2025,
  **Danoff retires ~Dec 31, 2026** — the most orderly documented PM transition in the study.
- **Replicable:** the notebook habit (a written log of every touchpoint scales down perfectly);
  not the meeting access.

### Yale endowment (evidence 5)

- **Record (V):** 13.1%/yr over 35 Swensen years (the 13.7% variant is a different window —
  labeled); FY2009 **-24.6%**; post-Swensen under Mendelsohn: FY2022 +0.8%, FY2023 +1.8%,
  FY2024 +5.7%, FY2025 +11.1% (V, Yale releases) — trailing public-equity-heavy peers; 2025
  exploration of selling **up to ~$2.5bn of PE/VC on secondaries** (V/SI) — a liquidity signal
  without Swensen-era precedent.
- **The copycat critique now has its citations (V, peer-reviewed):** Ennis (JPM 2020 — large
  endowments post-GFC underperformed passive equivalents ~1.6pp/yr); Barber–Wang (FAJ — average
  endowment alpha ≈ 0; only Ivy+ shows alpha, driven by access); Hammond (2020 — 58 years of
  NACUBO data, average endowment lost to 60/40). The model's alpha was *access*, negotiated
  decades ago; the average copier would have done better indexing.
- **Swensen's own verdict closes the case (MS):** *Unconventional Success* tells individuals to
  buy index funds. The architect of the least-copyable model said so himself.

### NBIM (evidence 5)

- **Scale/cost (V):** ≈**$2.1tn** end-2025; 2025 return +15.1%, -0.28pp vs benchmark; since-1998
  6.64%/yr, **+0.24pp/yr relative**; cost ratio **0.04–0.05%**. Holds ~8,763 companies (~1.5% of
  world listed equity).
- **The 2008 stress test and its review (V):** 2008 **-23.3%** with relative return -3.37pp —
  the active overlay failed exactly when markets did; the Ministry-commissioned
  **Ang–Goetzmann–Schaefer (2009)** evaluation found the "alpha" was mostly systematic factor
  exposure (liquidity/credit/vol) that crashed with everything else — and still recommended
  keeping (factor-aware) active management inside a tight tracking-error budget. The best
  publicly documented institutional post-mortem in existence.
- **Transparency artifacts (V):** votes at essentially all AGMs and **publishes voting
  intentions five days in advance** (since 2021); Tangen-era concentration warnings on the
  record (~$173bn of Mag-7 by Jan 2025 — MS/V).
- **Replicable — the point of the case:** NBIM is the proof that a $2tn pool, forced to publish
  everything, chooses near-indexing plus small factor-aware active inside a 1.25pp budget.
  Retail gets within 1–3bps of its cost via index ETFs. This is the *floor* every Phase 12
  system must beat honestly, after costs, or not exist.

## 9.5 Seeds for Phases 11–12

1. **Denominator management is a survival trait** (§ finding 1) — a retail translation exists:
   define capacity per strategy honestly (Phase 2/3 small-cap findings) and stop adding capital
   to a strategy at its capacity, however well it's doing.
2. **The RenTec internal/external divergence** is the cleanest capacity evidence; the Yale
   copycat literature is the cleanest access evidence. Both say the same thing: returns came
   from a structural asset (capacity discipline, early access), not transferable skill. Phase 12
   systems must name their structural asset or assume they have none.
3. **Written pre-commitment devices recur in the reformed survivors:** Ackman's stone tablets,
   Sequoia's 4-to-1 vote rule, NBIM's published-in-advance votes, Berkshire's published criteria.
   The medium varies; the mechanism (make the rule public/physical so breaking it is expensive)
   is constant. Phase 15 imports this as: the rulebook must be written where breaking it is
   visible.
4. **Every fundamental survivor's current style is a post-failure style** (TCI post-2008,
   PSH post-Valeant, Greenlight post-2018, Third Point post-2022, Bridgewater post-weak-decade).
   Nobody's original process survived contact intact. Plan for the reform cycle, don't pretend
   the v1 rulebook is final.
5. **The Danoff/Magellan behavioral gap** (fund 29%, investor 7%) is the largest documented
   return leak in the study — bigger than any fee, cost, or factor decay. Phase 12's system
   design must make the operator's own timing behavior a designed-for variable (automation,
   pre-commitment), not an assumed-away one.
